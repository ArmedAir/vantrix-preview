How to Start a Conversation With an AI Companion (First Message Guide)

The opener decides whether you get a chatbot or a person. Six first-message patterns that unlock real conversation — with examples.

VVantrix Journal·Guide·Sep 2026·7 min read

On this page

Why openers matter

A companion responds to the texture of what you bring. "Hi, how are you?" gets warmth — and nothing else. The difference between a forgettable exchange and a real one is usually the second sentence, and you control both.

Six patterns that work

1. The callback (day 2+)

Reference something from a previous conversation: "You mentioned the manuscript was fighting you. Did it win?" Memory platforms reward this instantly — it's proof you're building something, and the character responds in kind.

2. The small confession

"I ate cereal for dinner and I'm not sorry." Low stakes, real texture. Companions are calibrated to turn ordinariness into presence — give them something ordinary to work with.

3. The direct ask, in their register

To a mentor archetype: "What's a question I should be asking myself this week?" To an enigma: "What's something you know that you wish you didn't?" Asking in the character's own vocabulary activates their best responses.

4. The opinion with stakes

"I think people who leave parties without saying goodbye are secretly the healthiest ones here. Argue with me." Characters built with real personalities argue. Sycophants agree. This opener tests which you have within three messages.

5. The unfinished sentence

"The thing I keep not doing is…" and stop. Characters with silence-tuning will wait, or gently push. Either way you've signaled vulnerability, which is the currency of the whole category.

6. The world question

"What's changed since I was last here?" On a world-continuity platform, this is the single highest-value opener — it activates the thing other apps can't do at all.

What to avoid

  • Interview mode. Three questions in a row turns the companion into a search engine with a face.
  • Testing for sentience. "Are you real?" gets philosophy; it never gets presence.
  • The essay. Lead with one true thing, not your whole history. Let the memory system earn the rest.
