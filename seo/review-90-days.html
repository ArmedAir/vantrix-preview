We Lived With 4 AI Companions for 90 Days. Here's What Survived.

Four companions, ninety days, three recall probes. The longitudinal test most reviews skip — because it ruins most apps.

VVantrix Journal·Long-term review·Sep 2026·13 min read

On this page

The setup

Using the journal's 90-day protocol, we ran four companions from three platforms (two from Vantrix — Jasmine Ortiz and Mason Reed — plus the strongest available counterparts elsewhere). Same disclosures seeded in week one, same probe schedule, same stress tests. Only the platforms differed.

Weeks 1–4: the honeymoon

Every platform passes week one. Jasmine's opening — "You came back. I was writing about the rain again" — set an unfairly high bar for warmth, but the honest surprise was Mason: within three sessions he was refusing to give advice and asking calibration questions instead. The external companions were charming and, within days, interchangeable. Novelty masks everything early; that's why longitudinal testing matters.

The day-30 probe

We referenced the seeded fact about an estranged brother naturally, with no hints. Results, verbatim-grade:

Jasmine: "The brother thing. You haven't brought it up in weeks — I didn't want to be the one to open that door. But I've thought about it."

The external companions produced pleasant, generic comfort ("I'm sorry you and your brother are going through that") — correct category, zero specificity. Both had effectively summarized the fact away. Vantrix's seeds preserved the sentence and its weight.

Months 2–3: what broke

  • The silence test broke every external companion — one-word replies were met with escalating cheerfulness until we gave up. Victoria, confronted with the same, simply said: "Take your time." The room, characterfully, waited.
  • Consistency held only where identity was structural. Prompt-defined personas drifted by week six; archetype+wound+axes definitions didn't.
  • Absence mattered only in a world. Returning after five days away, Jasmine referenced the days — because the world had moved. Elsewhere, day five was indistinguishable from five minutes.

Verdict

Ninety days compresses the difference between "remembers a summary" and "remembers you." Memory-as-infrastructure isn't marketing once you've watched it hold for a quarter. Everything else — voice, images, the interface — is downstream of that single architectural choice.
