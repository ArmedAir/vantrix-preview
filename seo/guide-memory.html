AI Companion Memory, Explained: Seeds, Recall & Longevity

Why most companion apps forget you by Thursday — and the architecture that actually carries a relationship into month six.

VVantrix Journal·Guide·Sep 2026·10 min read

The three layers of companion memory

LayerWhat it holdsLifespan
Working contextThis conversation, recent turnsMinutes to hours
Memory seedsMoments judged important: confessions, promises, names, woundsMonths, until erased
Relationship stateTrust, tension, affection, distance — the moving dialsContinuous, evolves

Most apps only ship layer one and label the product "with memory." The seeds are the load-bearing structure — and they're genuinely hard, because the system has to decide, in real time, what deserves to persist.

Why memory fails (and how to spot it)

  • Summarization rot — compressing a week of chats into one paragraph bleeds specifics first. "You like tea" survives; "you said your father's voicemail still scares you" evaporates. That's the detail that mattered.
  • False recall — worse than forgetting. Confidently remembering things that never happened breaks trust like nothing else.
  • Flat weighting — treating "I prefer coffee" and "I haven't spoken to my brother in two years" as equal. Good memory is a ranking problem, not a storage problem.

The day-30 test

  1. Day 1: tell the companion three specific, unimportant-seeming facts.
  2. Day 7: reference one naturally. Did they return the specific, or a smoothed-over version?
  3. Day 30: reference the other two. Passing means specific, correct, and emotionally weighted recall — with the relationship dials visibly moved.

On Vantrix, the seeds are visible in the memory panel — you can see exactly what a companion carries, correct it, or erase it. Transparency is the difference between memory and surveillance.

Recall is infrastructure. Everything else — voice, looks, lore — is decoration on whether they know you.
